## Format
The exam is written (on paper) and split into three parts.

### 1. Multiple choice
Each question may have **one or more** correct answers; you mark the boxes in an answer grid. Scoring is designed so guessing is penalised:

- **+1** for each correctly selected option
- **−0.5** for each incorrectly selected option
- **0** for a correct option you did not select (no reward, no penalty)

Only marks inside the answer grid count. You can revise an answer by circling the box(es) you want: once anything is circled for a question, only the circled boxes are graded and all other marks for that question are ignored.

### 2. Open questions
Short free-text answers written on the provided lines. These test conceptual understanding; for example, explaining the difference between two techniques, or why one is preferable in a given situation. Guidance:

- **Answer the question directly** and precisely.
- **Be concise**; say what matters, leave out the irrelevant.
- **Legibility matters**; answers that can't be read can't be graded, and unclear handwriting may cost points.
- Full, grammatical sentences are preferred but not required: as long as your intent is clear, the answer counts.

### 3. Technical analysis (multi-step exploit riddles)
You analyse a technical artifact (a code snippet, a configuration, a scenario) and reason about its behaviour to derive the correct action or payload. Where a task has several subtasks, they **build on each other** unless stated otherwise: information from an earlier part may be needed later.
